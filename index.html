🌸 Women's Health Resources

Trusted information and contacts for women's physical and reproductive health in Bangladesh.

💬

Talking about periods is normal and important.

Menstrual health is a key part of overall wellbeing. Understanding your cycle helps you recognize what's healthy — and when to seek care.

📅

What is a Normal Cycle?

A typical menstrual cycle is 21–35 days long, with bleeding lasting 2–7 days. Flow can be light to heavy. Every woman's cycle is unique — tracking yours helps spot changes.

🌡️

Common Period Symptoms

Cramping (dysmenorrhea), bloating, mood changes, breast tenderness, and fatigue are common. Mild symptoms are normal. Severe pain that disrupts daily life is not — consult a doctor.

🩺

When to See a Doctor

Seek medical advice if you experience: periods lasting longer than 7 days, very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad/tampon every hour), severe cramping, irregular cycles, or periods stopping unexpectedly.

💊

Managing Period Pain

Ibuprofen or naproxen sodium (taken at the start of your period) can reduce cramps. Heat pads, light exercise, and staying hydrated also help. Talk to your doctor about hormonal options if pain is severe.

🍃

Nutrition & Your Cycle

Iron-rich foods (lentils, spinach, red meat) help replace what's lost during menstruation. Reducing salt, caffeine, and sugar may ease bloating. Vitamin D and calcium support hormonal balance.

🧘

Mental Health & Periods

PMS (premenstrual syndrome) and PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder) are real conditions. If mood changes before your period severely affect your quality of life, speak to a doctor or mental health professional.

🌸 Period Hygiene Tips

  • Change pads/tampons every 4–6 hours to prevent infection
  • Wash hands before and after changing sanitary products
  • Clean the vaginal area with water only — soap can disrupt pH
  • If using cloth pads, dry them in direct sunlight before use
  • Menstrual cups can be a cost-effective, eco-friendly option
  • Dispose of sanitary waste properly and hygienically
  • Period underwear is a reusable alternative worth considering
  • Track your cycle using an app (Clue, Flo) to notice irregularities
⚠️

Signs That Need Immediate Attention

Sudden severe pelvic pain, fever with heavy bleeding, foul-smelling discharge, or dizziness/fainting during periods — go to a hospital or call 199 for an ambulance.

💜

You are not alone — PCOS affects 1 in 10 women worldwide.

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is manageable. Early diagnosis and lifestyle changes can significantly improve quality of life.

🔬

What is PCOS?

PCOS is a hormonal disorder where the ovaries produce excess androgens (male hormones). This can cause irregular periods, cysts on the ovaries, and a range of physical symptoms. It is one of the most common causes of female infertility.

🔍

Common Symptoms

Irregular or missed periods · Excess hair on face/body (hirsutism) · Acne or oily skin · Thinning hair or hair loss · Weight gain, especially around the waist · Darkened skin patches · Difficulty getting pregnant

🏥

How is PCOS Diagnosed?

A doctor will review your symptoms, do a blood test (checking hormone levels, blood sugar, cholesterol), and may recommend a pelvic ultrasound to examine the ovaries. No single test confirms PCOS — it is diagnosed using a combination of criteria.

💊

Treatment Options

PCOS has no cure but is very manageable. Treatments include: combined oral contraceptive pills (to regulate periods and reduce androgens), Metformin (for insulin resistance), anti-androgen medications, and fertility treatments if pregnancy is desired.

🥗

Diet & Lifestyle for PCOS

A low glycemic index (low-GI) diet helps regulate blood sugar and hormones. Include: whole grains, lean protein, fruits, vegetables, and healthy fats. Reduce processed foods, sugar, and refined carbohydrates. Even 5–10% weight loss can restore regular periods.

🏃‍♀️

Exercise & PCOS

Regular moderate exercise (30 mins, 5 days/week) improves insulin sensitivity and reduces androgen levels. Strength training is especially beneficial. Yoga and meditation can help manage stress, which worsens PCOS symptoms.

🧠

PCOS & Mental Health

Women with PCOS have higher rates of anxiety and depression due to hormonal imbalances and the emotional burden of symptoms. If you're struggling emotionally, please speak to a mental health professional — this is a medical concern, not a personal weakness.

👶

PCOS & Fertility

PCOS is a leading cause of infertility, but many women with PCOS conceive with proper treatment. Options include Clomiphene (to induce ovulation), Letrozole, injectable gonadotropins, and IVF. Consult a reproductive endocrinologist for personalized guidance.

✦ Key Things to Tell Your Doctor

  • How long your periods are and how regular they are
  • Any recent weight changes, especially unexplained gain
  • Hair growth on face, chest, or stomach
  • Skin changes: acne, darkened patches, or skin tags
  • Any difficulty becoming pregnant (if relevant)
  • Family history of diabetes or PCOS
  • Current medications or supplements you are taking
  • Your stress levels and sleep quality
🌿

Family Planning

Multiple contraceptive options are available in Bangladesh including pills, IUDs, implants, and injectables. Consult a family planning clinic or government health center for free or low-cost access. Call 16767 for the National Family Planning Hotline.

🤰

Antenatal Care

Regular antenatal check-ups are essential during pregnancy. Bangladesh recommends at least 8 ANC visits. Government hospitals provide free ANC. Early registration ensures monitoring for complications like preeclampsia and gestational diabetes.

🎗️

Cervical & Breast Cancer Screening

Cervical cancer is preventable through regular Pap smears and HPV vaccination. Breast self-examination monthly and clinical breast exams annually are recommended. DGFP clinics and government hospitals offer free screening.

🔮

Menopause

Menopause typically occurs between ages 45–55. Symptoms include hot flashes, mood changes, sleep problems, and vaginal dryness. Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) and lifestyle changes can ease symptoms. Consult a gynaecologist for personalized care.

💉

STI Prevention & Testing

Sexually transmitted infections are common and often symptomless. Regular testing is important for sexually active women. Government health clinics offer confidential testing. HPV vaccination is available for girls aged 9–26.

🌸

Vaginal Health

Normal vaginal discharge varies throughout the cycle. Changes in color, smell, or texture may signal an infection like BV or yeast. Avoid douching. Wear breathable cotton underwear. See a doctor for persistent unusual discharge.

📖 How to Get Help

Step-by-step guidance for different emergency and support situations.

🚨

Immediate Physical Danger

Call 999 (Police) or 109 (National Helpline) immediately. If injured, call 199 for an ambulance. You do not need to explain everything — just say you need help and give your location.

🛖

You Need a Safe Place to Stay

Contact BNWLA (02-9143293) for their Proshanti Shelter, or ASK (01714-025069) for emergency accommodation. Call 109 to be connected to the nearest safe shelter in your district.

⚖️

You Need Legal Help

Call BLAST (01715-220220) for free legal representation. The National Legal Aid Hotline (16357) connects you to district-level legal aid. You do not need money to access legal support in Bangladesh.

🏥

After Sexual or Physical Violence

Go to the nearest One-Stop Crisis Centre (OCC) at a major hospital, or call 109 for a referral. OCCs provide medical care, forensic evidence collection, counselling, and legal assistance together. You do not need a police report to access care.

💙

You Need Emotional Support

Call Kaan Pete Roi (01779-554391) for confidential emotional support. For ongoing counselling, contact Moner Bondhu or ASK's psychosocial team (01724-415677). You don't have to go through this alone.

🔗

Trafficking or Forced Marriage

Call 109 immediately. Contact BNWLA (02-9143293) for specialized anti-trafficking support, or Bandhu Social Welfare (01714-048418). All calls are confidential and free of charge.

What to Do in an Emergency

1

If you are in immediate danger, call Police 999 or National Helpline 109 first.

2

If you need a safe place to stay, contact BNWLA or ASK for emergency shelter.

3

For free legal help, call BLAST (01715-220220) or National Legal Aid 16357.

4

For medical care after violence, go to the nearest OCC hospital or call 109 to be connected.
